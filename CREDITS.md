# Credits, origin and intellectual property

This document lists everything that makes up Paint the Music and where it came
from. It is meant to be published along with the project.

---

## Who did what

**Design, code, graphics and music**: produced for this project. Rights in the
whole belong to the project's author.

**The interface was built with Claude**, Anthropic's AI assistant. The brief,
the direction and every decision about what the app should be are the author's.

Anthropic claims no ownership of what Claude helps produce: there is no licence
to reproduce and no attribution owed on that account. Worth knowing when
settling the licence, though: copyright in several jurisdictions, notably the
United States, protects only what a human author contributed, which argues for
a permissive licence rather than a restrictive one.

**Nothing has been taken from a third party.** Not one line of code, not an
image, not a sound, not a font.

---

## Genre and prior art

Paint the Music belongs to a long-established family of instruments: the
**grid sequencer**, where time runs horizontally and pitch vertically. The
idea is decades old piano rolls, step drum machines, trackers, and the
countless piano-roll editors of music software. Laying a painting metaphor
over it is no newer.

There is therefore **no single source to credit**: neither the grid principle,
nor the scale constraint, nor the gesture of drawing in order to play is
anyone's invention.

What the law actually turns on, and where the position here is unambiguous:

- **An idea, a feature or an interaction principle is not protected by
  copyright** only its concrete expression is: code, graphics, text, sounds,
  mock-ups.
- **No third party's expression has been reused.** Not a line of code, not a
  stylesheet, not an image, not a sound, not a sentence. The synthesis engine,
  the interface, the palette and the file format were all designed for this
  project.
- **No affiliation with any other software or service whatsoever.**

When writing about the app, stick to what it does. **Do not use another
product's name, logo or look**, including to compare against it: nothing
requires it, and it would needlessly suggest a connection between the two.

## Dependencies: none

The project contains **no third-party library**, no npm package, no remote
script. Nothing is loaded from a CDN. There is therefore **no third-party
licence to honour or to reproduce** a rare and comfortable position for an
open-source release.
